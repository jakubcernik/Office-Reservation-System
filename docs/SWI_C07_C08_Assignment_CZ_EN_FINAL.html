A. Technical Design Review – na příští cvičení

Účel: přesvědčivě ukázat jednu konzistentní cestu od požadavku a rozhodnutí až k implementaci, verifikaci a reakci na nezávislý assessment. Nejde o soutěž množství slidů; prezentujte skutečně hotové technické výsledky a přiznejte omezení.

Forma: přibližně 8–10 min prezentace a technické dotazy; stačí otevřený repozitář, existující diagramy, běžící aplikace/testy nebo stručné podpůrné slidy. Nevyžaduje se nový rozsáhlý deck.

Co znamená sloupec „Verified“ na status slidu?

Specified: přijaté a konzistentní chování ve specifikaci v0.2. Designed: zodpovědnost, interaction a relevantní DD jsou explicitní a aktuální. Implemented: funkce v identifikovaném commitu skutečně existuje a lze ji spustit. Verified: existuje odpovídající evidence s nezávislým oracle, očekávaným a pozorovaným výsledkem; pokud test nebyl spuštěn nebo neověřuje claim, neoznačujte Verified.

Příklad překladu technického findingu do statusu

Technical: „C06 finding F2: dva konfliktní confirm requesty mohou oba skončit CONFIRMED.“
Project impact: „Confirm je Implemented, ale NOT VERIFIED vůči concurrency claimu. Risk R-03 HIGH; mitigation: transactional guard + concurrency integration test; owner Petr, due 4. 11.; expected residual MEDIUM, actual residual NOT VERIFIED; overall project AT RISK.“

Today: C06 assessment findings → verified decisions

Input: the C06 assessment performed by another team, your assessed baseline (commit/tag), documentation, code and tests. C06 evaluated only the actual obligations of C01–C05. Do not add retrospective requirements or accept a finding just because the reviewers prefer a different technology.

Procedure for each significant candidate finding

  1. Claim: which specific claim or requirement from C01–C05 is allegedly violated? Provide its ID or location in the documentation.
  2. Expected: what should have happened according to the accepted specification, design or assignment?
  3. Observed: what did the reviewers actually find? Provide a file path, test, log or reproduction steps.
  4. Verify: confirm the finding against the identified baseline; if the matter is interpretative, compare the relevant artifacts.
  5. Disposition: VERIFIED FINDING / REJECTED CANDIDATE, followed by ACCEPT / REJECT / DEFER, with rationale, owner, and action or justified no-change.
  6. Closure evidence: if it was fixed, show the repository change and test/review evidence. Otherwise, treat it as an open issue or risk, not as resolved.

A. Technical Design Review – for the next exercise

Purpose: demonstrate one consistent chain from a requirement and engineering decision through implementation, verification and response to independent assessment. This is not a competition to produce many slides: present real technical results and acknowledge limitations.

Format: approximately 8–10 minutes plus technical questions. An open repository, existing diagrams, a running application/tests, or a few supporting slides are sufficient. A large new slide deck is not required.

Example: one consistent technical argument

REQ-04 + BR-02: two conflicting CONFIRMED reservations must not exist for the same exclusive Resource → Architecture: Reservation Management owns the confirmation decision, protected by an appropriate consistency boundary → Detailed Design: Confirm validates state, checks conflict, updates state atomically and persists the result → Evidence: adjacent intervals allowed; conflicts rejected; concurrent confirmations never produce two confirmations → C06 finding: if review identified an unprotected admin/approval path, explain and substantiate the disposition and planned action.

Important: hypothetical administrator override or direct database import are not automatically required features of your application. Treat them as risks only if these paths actually exist or are realistically planned. Do not turn examples into new C01–C05 obligations.

B. Risk Assessment – prepare for the next exercise

Purpose: identify uncertainty that may threaten your project completion and quality, decide how to respond, and substantiate the actual situation. Use current C06 findings, remaining work, dependencies, capacities and quality evidence. Do not compile a generic risk list unrelated to your project.

B1. Risk versus related concepts

TermDefinitionConcrete example
RiskAn uncertain event that could affect an objectiveIf the only capable team member is unavailable, integration may be delayed
IssueA problem that has already occurredThe current build cannot be run on a second computer
Defect / FindingA demonstrated nonconformity with an accepted claimThe real test fails to reject a conflicting reservation
AssumptionSomething treated as true without certaintyThe external service will be available during the defence

B2. Examine at least three areas of the project

CategoryWhat to examineExample risk
Scope / RequirementsAmbiguous rule, change in approval, incorrect assumptionsUnclear PENDING_APPROVAL policy causes rework
Schedule / IntegrationMilestones, dependencies, late integrationChanges are integrated only just before CP2 → no time remains for fixes
People / ResourcesAvailability, substitutability and key knowledgeOnly one person knows the database configuration
Technical / QualityInvariants, state transitions, concurrency, quality criterionConcurrent confirmation causes double booking
External dependencyNotification service, identity, database, environmentsA service outage blocks a critical scenario
Security / PrivacyRoles, admin permissions, personal data and loggingAn overly broad role enables unauthorized reservation changes
Release / OperationReproducible build, configuration, rollback, monitoringThe application cannot run outside the development laptop

Scope: at least four concrete risks from at least three categories. For each risk, write cause → uncertain event → consequence; for example, “Only one developer maintains DB configuration → nobody can start the application if they are absent → integration and the CP2 demonstration are delayed.”

B3. Common classification (before treatment)

Likelihood (L)Meaning
1 – LowThe event is less likely; there is no known concrete warning signal or it is not very realistic
2 – MediumA realistic possibility; indicators, dependencies or uncertainty exist
3 – HighA highly realistic possibility; strong warnings or repeated prior occurrence
Impact (I)Meaning for the project
1 – LowSmall, local impact that can be handled without affecting an essential objective
2 – MediumSignificant effect on scope, schedule, quality or capacity, but recovery is realistic
3 – HighThreatens a key business invariant, a useful demonstration, the release or a critical deadline

The L × I score helps prioritize attention; it is not an exact prediction. A major business, safety or security consequence may need action even when likelihood is low. Briefly justify each selected L and I value.

B5. Risk response and residual risk

StrategyMeaningExample
AVOIDChange the plan or scope so that the risky scenario cannot occurDo not depend on an unreliable external integration for CP2 if it is not essential
MITIGATEReduce likelihood or impact by a concrete actionAutomated integration test plus an owner backup
TRANSFER / SHARETransfer or share some consequences/responsibilities with another party without escaping project accountabilityArrange a backup operating service with a clear SLA, if available
ACCEPTConsciously accept the remaining risk with rationale and monitoring; may include a contingency reserveA minor visual UI limitation is acceptable for CP2

Initial risk = risk before treatment. Expected residual = estimate after the planned treatment. Actual residual = only after treatment has been implemented and its effectiveness verified. State NOT VERIFIED when treatment has not yet been verified; never reduce the score merely because an action has been planned. Acceptance owner = identified person/team who justifies the decision. Trigger = observable signal that requires revisiting the risk.

C. Project Status Report – for the next exercise

Purpose: give a short, decision-ready account of the project’s actual status. In a professional automotive-style project, a status report is not an activity log: it is an evidenced view of scope, deliverables, schedule, resources, risks and decisions needed. Use the provided editable six-slide PPTX template (separate file in the package). Enter only real data; do not invent percentages without a defined denominator.

Presentation: 5–7 minutes plus questions. Reporting baseline = the date and commit/tag for which all claims are true. Technical details and code belong in part A; here, report their actual project impact.

How to fill slide 02: in each Specified / Designed / Implemented / Verified cell, enter YES / PARTLY / NO, together with a link to the supporting evidence or gap. Use N/A with a justification only for a genuinely inapplicable item. Do not mark an item Verified automatically. A known confirmed defect is recorded among open issues, not only as a possible future risk.

If a plan or forecast does not yet exist, report that planning gap and name who will resolve it and by when; do not invent historical dates or completion percentages.

The template has four priority-action rows on slide 05. Show the four most important actions there; keep any additional action in the repository/work plan and link to it rather than overcrowding the slide.

Unified RAG classification

StatusWhen to use it
GREEN / ON TRACKAgreed scope and deadline remain realistic, critical evidence exists and no major unresolved impediment remains
AMBER / AT RISKDelivery is still possible, but a significant uncertainty, capacity issue or technical obstacle requires action or decision
RED / OFF TRACKUnder the current plan, key scope, deadline or quality cannot be met without a changed decision or intervention

What does “Verified” mean on the status slide?

Specified: accepted, consistent behavior in specification v0.2. Designed: ownership, interaction and relevant DD are explicit and current. Implemented: the capability actually exists at the stated commit and can run. Verified: corresponding evidence exists with an independent oracle and expected/observed results; if the test was not executed or does not verify the claim, do not mark it Verified.

Example: translate a technical finding into project status

Technical: “C06 finding F2: two conflicting Confirm requests can both end in CONFIRMED.”
Project impact: “Confirm is Implemented, but NOT VERIFIED against the concurrency claim. Risk R-03 HIGH; mitigation: transactional guard + concurrency integration test; owner Petr; due 4 Nov.; expected residual MEDIUM, actual residual NOT VERIFIED; overall project AT RISK.”
